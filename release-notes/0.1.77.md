Security has a new **IP blocks** page: one list of every address the server refuses, with blocking and unblocking in bulk, a list of allowed addresses that nothing can block, a page for each address that shows which sites it visited, blocking by country, and a 90-day history.

- **Where:** Security → **IP blocks** (the button at the top right of Security shows how many blocks are in force), or Ctrl+K. The table of blocked addresses under Live Monitor → Attacks moved to this page; Live Monitor now shows their number with a link, and a suspect's address there opens that address's page. Administrators only.
- **Blocked addresses:** every block in one list, whoever made it: by hand, Serfex AI, the API or Fail2ban. Each row shows the country, the reason, the site it was blocked on, who blocked it, what the block covers (the whole server, or for a Fail2ban ban only the ports of the attacked service) and the time left. Filter by who blocked, search by address, reason, site or country, sort by newest or ending soonest. Searching for an address also finds the blocked range that holds it.
- **Blocking in bulk:** **Block IP** takes one address or a pasted list: one per line or separated by commas, spaces or semicolons, with `#` for comments, up to 1,000 at a time. Before anything is blocked the dialog says how many are new, how many are blocked already (their duration is changed) and which are left out and why: not an address, a range wider than /16 (IPv6 /48), your own address, the server's own addresses, Cloudflare's addresses, an allowed address, or an overlap with an existing block. Durations are 1 hour, 24 hours, 7 days, 30 days or permanent.
- **Multiple selection:** tick rows, the whole page, or **Select all N addresses in the list**, then **Unblock**, **Change duration** or **Add to allowed** in one step. Changing the duration of a Fail2ban ban turns it into a block on the whole server.
- **Undo:** the message of every action has **Undo** for 10 minutes. An undone bulk block is lifted and leaves nothing in History; an undone unblock comes back for the time it had left.
- **Which sites an address visited:** click an address to see, per site, the number of requests, the path asked for most, the answers by status class, refused requests and the last request, then the newest 200 requests (narrowed by site, answer and path) and every block the address has had. The numbers are read from the sites' access logs when the page opens, as far back as the logs are kept (14 days by default); on very large logs the reading stops after 20 seconds and the page says so. Requests that came through Cloudflare are counted under the visitor's real address. The same page blocks, unblocks, changes the duration and allows the address.
- **Countries:** the Countries tab lists every country with the requests and visitors it sent your sites in the last 30 days, and **Block a country** refuses the ones you choose. One scope holds for all of them: **Websites only** (the default) answers their visitors with 403 on every site, also behind Cloudflare's proxy, and leaves mail, SSH, FTP, the panel and Let's Encrypt renewals alone; **The whole server** also drops their new connections in the firewall, except to the web ports, DNS (53) and incoming mail (25), so mail from those countries still arrives. Allowed addresses, the server's own addresses and Cloudflare's addresses always pass; the country you are connected from cannot be blocked; the United States get a warning because search engines and most payment services connect from there. A country is where an address is registered (the regional internet registries' lists the site statistics already use, refreshed monthly), not a location service, so VPNs and cloud providers can fall under another country.
- **Allowed addresses:** addresses and ranges that are never blocked: not by hand, not by Serfex AI, not by Fail2ban, not by a country block and not by a wider block that holds them. Adding an address lifts its blocks at once. The server's own addresses are always on the list; the address you are connected from is marked. The list is applied in the firewall before the blocked addresses, and is written to Nginx and to Fail2ban's `ignoreip`.
- **History:** blocks that ended are kept for 90 days with who blocked, why, for how long and how the block ended. **Block again** blocks an address for as long as before, one row or several together.
- **Locked out:** your own address and the server's addresses cannot be blocked from the panel. If you are kept out anyway, run `sudo serfex firewall unblock <address>` on the server (over SSH, or from your provider's console): it lifts the block on the address, a blocked range that holds it and Fail2ban's bans. If the country you are in was blocked, `sudo serfex firewall countries off` lifts every country block.
- **Existing servers:** the blocks in force stay as they are and appear on the new page. Nothing else changes by itself: Nginx and Fail2ban are rewritten only when you add the first allowed address, and no country is blocked until you block one.
